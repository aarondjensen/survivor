How the score is built. Path is the chance of running the table — this week’s win probability times the best assignment of the teams you have left to the weeks you have left, so spending a team here is priced by what it costs there. Lev is how much of the pool you own if you survive: the expected value of 1 / (surviving share) given your team wins, as a multiple of your fair share. It is taken over the field’s whole distribution and not its average, because the branch that pays is the one where the popular pick loses and a sliver of the pool is left holding your ticket — an average survivor count erases exactly that branch. Score is Path × Levλ.

λ = 1.00 is not a weighting, it is the objective. There the score multiplies out to rest-of-season survival times this week’s expected equity, which is the thing you are trying to maximise. Drag it above 1 to lean harder into differentiation than one week of EV justifies — defensible, because survivor is multi-week and winner-take-all and this prices one week. Nothing here is fitted to results.

The numbers loaded now are a sample season unless you have imported your own: a synthetic round-robin slate with real team names, priced off the sample ratings above. It is shaped like an NFL season — 17 games, one bye each between weeks 5 and 14 — so the board behaves correctly, but no game on it is a real fixture and no probability is anyone’s published forecast.
